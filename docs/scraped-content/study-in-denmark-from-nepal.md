## Denmark Student Visa Requirements for Nepalese Students

The section explains the Danish Long-Stay Student Visa (Type D) as a "Residence Permit for Higher Education."

**Required Documents:**
- Admission Letter from recognized Danish university
- ST1 Application Form (filled jointly by student and institution)
- Valid passport for entire study duration
- Financial proof (approximately DKK 76,764/year or NPR 15,00,000+)
- English proficiency (IELTS/TOEFL/PTE scores)
- Accommodation proof/rental agreement
- Travel & health insurance
- Passport-size photos (35mm x 45mm)
- Biometric data (fingerprints and photo)
- Visa fee (approximately DKK 1,890)

**Processing Time:** "2 to 3 months. Apply early to avoid delays."

## FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

**Q: Why should Nepalese students choose Denmark for higher education?**

A: "Denmark offers world-class education, globally recognized degrees, innovative teaching methods, and excellent post-study work opportunities. It's known for its high quality of life, student-friendly cities, and strong English-taught programs."

**Q: Do I need to speak Danish to study in Denmark?**

A: "No. Many Bachelor's and Master's programs are taught entirely in English. However, learning basic Danish can be helpful for daily life and part-time job opportunities."

**Q: What are the English language requirements for Nepali students?**

A: Requirements typically include:
- IELTS: 6.5 overall (no band less than 6.0)
- TOEFL iBT: 83–100
- PTE: 58–65
- Requirements may vary by university and program

**Q: What are the intakes to apply for universities in Denmark?**

A: "The primary intake is Autumn (September). Some universities also offer a Spring (February) intake for select programs."

**Q: How much does it cost to study in Denmark for Nepali students?**

A:
- Tuition Fees: NPR 9 – 30+ Lakhs/year
- Living Expenses: NPR 12 – 18 Lakhs/year
- Scholarships available to help reduce costs

**Q: Are there scholarships available for Nepalese students in Denmark?**

A: "Yes. Nepali students can apply for Danish Government Scholarships, Erasmus+ Scholarships, University-specific merit scholarships, and Private foundation or alumni scholarships."

**Q: Can I work while studying in Denmark?**

A: "Yes. International students can work 20 hours per week during the semester and full-time during holidays."

**Q: Can I stay in Denmark after graduation?**

A: "Yes. After completing your studies, you can apply for a 6-month post-study job search visa, and later for a work visa if you find employment."

**Q: What are the student visa requirements for Nepalese students?**

A: You'll need:
- Admission letter from Danish university
- ST1 visa application form
- Proof of funds (~NPR 15+ Lakhs/year)
- Health insurance, accommodation, and biometric data
